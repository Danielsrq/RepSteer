Reading & writing concepts in a 4B LLM

Activation steering / mechanistic-interpretability playground · Qwen3.5-4B · single RTX 3080 Ti (12 GB)

Curated results — the four threads worth showing. Each card links to its interactive figure.

Three families of method, one question: where do concepts live in the residual stream, and can we read them and write them? We start with difference-of-means (DiM) directions on an emotion testbed, localize concepts with the Jacobian lens / J-space, and train LoReFT low-rank interventions — which give a read (qi) and write (pi) channel from one object. The recurring theme is the gap between reading a concept and writing it.

ref: Representation Engineering / persona vectors I · DiM

Grand-mean DiM over emotion vectors

Twelve emotion concepts, each a direction = concept mean − grand mean (grand mean over the 11 emotional concepts, neutral excluded). Every token of a paragraph is projected onto all concepts and tinted by its argmax; a steering tab pushes generation toward any concept.

96.1% nearest-concept accuracy (94.2% LOO). Intuitive cosine structure — only angry↔belligerent confuse. Steering sweet spot α ≈ ±0.3–0.5 (norm-relative).

ref: Jacobian-lens / J-space II · J-space

J-space occupancy: layer sweep & vs model size

Fit a Jacobian lens across a layer band, then measure J-space occupancy — how many verbalizable concept directions a token's residual decomposes into (non-negative OMP vs a same-size random control) — per layer, for the 4B and a 2B model at matched relative depth.

Occupancy rises with depth and peaks at L25 (~19) vs L17's ~9. The bigger model has larger raw occupancy, but most of the gap is the d_model confound — a modest genuine edge survives only at the peak.

ref: ReFT (Wu et al.) III · LoReFT

Low-rank interventions: writing, and reading via V = W − R

LoReFT learns a rank-4 intervention that overwrites a small subspace (unlike additive DiM, which superimposes). SVD of V = W − R splits it into a read subspace qi and a write subspace pi — so one trained object is both sensor and actuator.

III-1 · Emoji: DiM steering attempts, and why the LoReFT drops the system prompt the training-context lesson

A tabbed figure. Tab 1 is the head-to-head of the DiM steering vectors — naive byte-DiM, the captured system-prompt conditioning offset, and LoReFT — each swept to its best strength. (Four dead-end variants are folded away behind a toggle, with a note on why each failed.) Tab 2 then trains the LoReFT two ways (with vs without the system prompt in its context) and steers a bare question with each.

Naive DiM fails or stays weak — byte-DiM barely emits emoji, and only the conditioning offset works at all (~29–52%). LoReFT reaches 99%. And it must be trained without the system prompt: with-sys starts at loss 0.44 → learns nothing (‖Δ‖ 8, 0% emoji), no-sys starts at 1.94 → 99% (‖Δ‖ 25). With the prompt present there is no gradient.
III-2 · LoReFT vs DiM steering (happy / sad) read ≠ write

Three prompts × five conditions, five selectable readers, a last/all scheme toggle. DiM steers all positions continuously; LoReFT steers prompt positions at prefill.

For a smooth concept like tone, DiM steers ≥ LoReFT, especially at overriding a contrary prompt — on a sad prompt, happy-DiM flips it (+1.5) while happy-LoReFT can't (−0.8). LoReFT all > last, but prompt-only still can't match DiM's continuous scope over a long generation. (Opposite of emoji, where LoReFT won.)

ref: DiM + LoReFT + J-space, on real CVEs IV · Applied

Reading a real security corpus: 5-label readers + J-space wordcloud

The methods fused on real data: four held-out CVE write-ups, every token tinted by a switchable 5-way reader (DiM · full-space LDA · LoReFT read qi · write pi) under the background / triage / overflow / uaf / race scheme, with a per-token J-space wordcloud side panel (lens refit on the clean corpus; L17 / L25 / L27 selectable by occupancy). A prediction/ground-truth toggle checks the readers against hand labels.

A 4B reads narrative role well (background/triage) but bug mechanism poorly: race is best via the LoReFT read subspace (0.68), overflow via full LDA (0.72), and uaf is unreadable by every method. The J-space decompositions are on-concept and multilingual (reconstruction doubles L17→L25).
