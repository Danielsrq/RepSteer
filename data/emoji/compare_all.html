Notes & caveats

  • All static vectors are a single fixed direction added to the residual stream; LoReFT is a learned, input-dependent edit of a 4-dimensional subspace applied at one position (the last prompt token). Its effect on later tokens flows only through the model's own layers.
  • The system-token rows rescale their direction to the norm of the #2 offset so magnitudes are comparable; their raw activation is ~60% unrelated to the emoji offset (cos 0.41) and tends to inject <think> scaffolding instead.
  • Metrics are crude: overlap penalises valid but different emoji choices, and diversity rewards variety even when it drifts off-topic. Read the per-prompt outputs, not just the numbers.
  • Held-out prompts' true answers exist in emoji_generated.json but were never trained on; fresh prompts appear nowhere. The static α choice was tuned on all 10 prompts.

Why these four are folded (they didn't work). Each was a plausible way to bottle the system prompt into a vector, and each fails as a usable steering direction: #2 + sampling & repetition penalty buys diversity but not emoji (25% emoji, incoherent — the penalty just scatters tokens); #3 generated-response DiM collapses into � replacement-char spam (15% emoji, diversity ~0); and the system-token vectors are raw activations, not contrasts — they inject <think> scaffolding rather than emoji (N=1 cos 0.41 with the real offset; mean-centring lifts cos to 0.73 but drops emoji to 3%). The lesson is the one that carries the whole story: a raw activation is not a steering direction — only a contrast (#2, or better, the learned LoReFT edit) is. Tick the box above to inspect them.
