Reading vulnerability mechanisms off real CVE write-ups

Qwen3.5-4B, layer 17. 19 published write-ups, span-labelled and collapsed into contiguous regions. nftables + smbghost held out entirely. This replaces the synthetic security corpus.

2. Reading regions in isolation

Every direction is fitted on token-level residuals and scored by AUC. LOAO refits inside each leave-one-article-out fold; HOLD fits on all 17 training articles and scores nftables + smbghost. The negative pool is varied from easy (plain background prose) to hard (exploit text, which is also about memory corruption, and other mechanism classes).

Fit the direction inside the fold. Fitting the difference-of-means once on all training positives and then scoring folds leaks the held-out article into the direction. For uaf that alone moved the leave-one-article-out AUC from 0.63 to 0.82. Every number below refits per fold.

3. Reading the article as a stream — and why it differs

The numbers above do not describe the viewer. Section 2 forwards each region on its own. The token viewer forwards a continuous 2512-token window of the article, so every layer-17 residual carries the whole preceding context. Same text, different distribution — and a direction fitted on isolated regions scores at chance on the streaming page. 4. What this says

  • Real data fixed the transfer failure. race_condition reads at 0.75 (DiM) and 0.85 (full LDA) on the held-out articles, against 0.95 when the negatives are plain prose. The synthetic-corpus readers were at chance on the same text. It is also the class with the most real data — 19 regions across 4 articles.
  • The rank-4 LoReFT is the wrong instrument for these concepts. Its read and write channels land between 0.33 and 0.73, mostly inside or below the random rank-4 band, and its read directions are near-orthogonal to the data direction (cos(qi, DiM) ≈ 0.03–0.05). This holds even though the intervention trains fine — the classify objective drives loss from 0.105 to 0.005. It learns to answer the question without routing the answer through a direction that is linearly readable off other text.
  • Difference-of-means is measuring "vulnerability-flavoured text", not the mechanism. Against plain prose uaf DiM reads 0.63; against other mechanism classes it reads 0.455, and on the holdout 0.200 — below chance, meaning other mechanisms score higher on the "uaf" direction than uaf does. The same inversion hits heap_overflow (0.247) and oob_access (0.373). A single mean-difference direction cannot separate mechanisms from each other.
  • The information is there; the rank-4 probe is what is missing. A full-space LDA beats DiM almost everywhere and survives hard negatives where DiM inverts — uaf 0.771 vs exploit text on the holdout, command_injection 0.755–0.769 across every pool, uninit_memory 0.805. So layer 17 does linearly encode the distinction; four directions chosen by a steering objective are not the four that carry it.
  • Class count is the binding constraint, not article count. The classes that work (race_condition 19 regions, uninit_memory 7, command_injection 8) are the ones with regions spread over 3–4 articles. oob_access (5 regions) is at chance on every pool.
Caveat on the labels. The spans were labelled by agents following data/security/LABELLING_GUIDE.md, not by a security expert, and only spot-checked. nftables and cve_2022_0185 had to be re-fetched and re-labelled after their first HTML→text extraction destroyed paragraph structure — that defect alone had collapsed nftables into 6 regions, one of them 76 kB.
